# The founding request for translation

Why this exists: the translation work in Trellis began with one public
post. It is kept here, word for word as the owner gave it to the project,
so that the work can always be judged against what was asked for and not
against what was easiest to build.

## The post

Devon Zuegel (@devonzugel on X), 17 May 2025:

> I would pay for a podcast player that automatically translates episodes
> into other languages so that I could practice Spanish while listening to
> my favorite shows. I would build it myself but too busy right now, so
> putting this out in the ether in hopes that someone else does!

The follow-up, same thread:

> Bonus points if I can choose how much they enunciate, ranging from
> totally colloquial street speech to as crisp and clean as possible to
> make it easy for a beginner to parse

And one exchange under it. Someone asked:

> English/Spanish voice so you can listen in Spanish or English with
> subtitles in Spanish?

Devon Zuegel answered:

> Spanish voice! Do podcasts support subtitles?

(All recorded on 2026-11-10 from the owner's notes to the project. The
posts themselves were not opened when this page was written.)

## What it asks for

- **A podcast player.** Not a translation tool with a file box: the
  thing you already listen in.
- **Automatic translation of whole episodes.** Not a sentence at a time,
  and not something to set up again for each episode.
- **The listener's favourite shows.** Any feed they follow, not a
  catalogue chosen for them.
- **Language practice.** The aim is to hear, and follow, another
  language. Spanish is the first case.
- **A control for how clearly it is spoken** (the follow-up, as a bonus).
  One thing the listener sets, from casual everyday speech to speech as
  crisp and clean as possible, so a beginner can pick the words apart.
  It is about the translated voice, and it reaches into two things at
  once: how the words are said, and which words are chosen.

## What of the follow-up is met

How clearly the voice speaks is a setting (Settings › Learning), with
four steps from Natural to Very slow. Each slows the saved voice and
lengthens the pause after each sentence; the downloaded voice also takes
more care over each sound. That covers the crisp and clean end, for both
voices, on every episode made after the choice. The other end,
"totally colloquial street speech", is not met: both voices read the
words they are given in one register and cannot sound more casual than
they do. It would take a casual translation (other words, not only
other sounds) and a voice trained on casual speech. Neither has been
heard on a phone yet. The steps and their numbers are in the feature
matrix.

## What the exchange settles

- **The wanted output is the spoken voice.** The episode is heard in
  Spanish. English audio with Spanish words on screen is not what was
  asked for.
- **Words on screen are not expected.** The person asking did not know a
  podcast player could show the words at all. So the lit transcript in
  Trellis is something to show a first-time listener, plainly and
  early, not something to assume they will look for.

## The flow this makes the main case

1. An English podcast episode, from a show the listener follows.
2. A transcript made on the device (whisper).
3. Translation from English into the listener's language.
4. The episode **spoken** in that language, with the words lit as they
   are read.

English into another language is the first direction. Another language
into English is the second.

## How to use this page

Any change to transcription, translation or read aloud is judged against
this flow from start to end, on a real episode of about an hour: how many
steps the listener takes by hand, how long each stage runs, what happens
when the phone sleeps or the app is closed partway, and whether the
result is something a person can practise a language with. A stage that
works alone but leaves the listener to join the stages by hand has not
met the request.
